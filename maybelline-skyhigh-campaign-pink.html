01 — The Problem

Sky High is known.
But it isn't felt.

Maybelline Sky High Mascara holds strong emotional recall among women aged 28 and above — many of whom were first introduced to makeup through Maybelline during their school or early college years. For this audience, the mascara represents more than a cosmetic. It symbolises their first step into self-expression, confidence, and everyday beauty rituals.

But this legacy narrative has never been effectively communicated to younger consumers. Gen Z encounters the product primarily through trend-driven algorithm-led content — not through its emotional heritage. As a result, Maybelline risks losing an entire generation's loyalty to indie brands and K-beauty players who speak directly to her dreams.

The challenge isn't awareness. Sky High is known. The challenge is emotional ownership — making her feel like this mascara was made for who she is becoming, not just who her mother was.

The strategic question
How can Maybelline reposition Sky High Mascara to bridge its emotional legacy with contemporary beauty culture — so that Gen Z consumers connect with it not just as a trending makeup item, but as a symbol of confidence, self-expression, and generational ambition?
02 — Target Audience

Two women looking at
the same sky.

This campaign doesn't choose between legacy and Gen Z. It holds both. Because these two women want the same thing — they just stand at different points on the same journey.

01
The 18-Year-Old Dreamer
Gen Z · 18–24 · Metro India
She lies on the terrace at night, looking at a sky full of stars — and she wants every single one of them. She is ambitious, bold, brimming with confidence, but she's still at the beginning of her runway. She hasn't taken off yet. She just needs that sky-high jump — and she'll have all those stars in her hand. Maybelline has never spoken directly to her hunger. This campaign does.
"The stars aren't far. They're just waiting for me to reach."
02
The 35-Year-Old Achiever
Working woman · 28–35 · The one she wanted to become
She sits in a conference room. To the world, she has arrived — fierce, respected, the ideal figure that 18-year-old on the terrace dreams of becoming. But then she walks to the glass window and looks at the sky full of stars again. And she realises: there are still stars she hasn't touched. The calmness she wants. The stillness. The respect that goes beyond titles.
"I've achieved what I chased. Now I'm learning what else there is to chase."
"Maybelline just doesn't need an introduction like a new product. It was part of every vanity for years. It just needs to show that it supports the vision of Gen Z."
The big idea

A sky full of stars.
Two women.
One mascara.
No ceiling.

The entire campaign is built around one universal, timeless image: a sky full of stars. For the 18-year-old, those stars are dreams she hasn't reached yet — ambitions sitting just above her, waiting to be claimed. For the 35-year-old, those same stars are the reminder that even after everything she's built, there is still more sky above her. Sky High doesn't ask you to stop at the sky. It asks — what's beyond it?

03 — Hero Film Treatment

The 60-second film —
"Same Sky, Different Stars"

A cinematic film that intercuts between two women on the same night — looking at the same sky, dreaming different things. Shot in rich, moody blues and warm golds. No voiceover until the final moment. Just the sky, the stars, and her eyes.

Scene by scene — "Same Sky, Different Stars"
0:00 – 0:12 · Scene 1 — The Dreamer
A terrace. Night. Stars everywhere.
An 18-year-old woman — fresh face, bright eyes, the whole sky in front of her. She lies on the terrace of her home, looking upward. Her sketchbook beside her. Her dreams just above. She traces a star with her finger. She smiles — not because she has arrived. Because she knows she's about to.
0:12 – 0:25 · Scene 2 — The Achiever
A glass boardroom. Night. City lights below.
A 35-year-old woman — Smriti Mandhana energy. Composed, fierce, respected. The conference room has emptied. She stands at the glass wall, city below her. Then her gaze lifts — past the buildings, past the lights. To the stars. Her expression isn't satisfied. It's still hungry.
0:25 – 0:45 · Scene 3 — The Mascara Moment
The same gesture. Two mirrors. One truth.
Intercut: both women, in front of their mirrors. The same movement — mascara wand, one confident stroke upward. The 18-year-old getting ready for her first big moment. The 35-year-old preparing for whatever comes next. Their eyes meet their own reflection. Something shifts — not transformation, but recognition. I know who I am. And I know I'm not done yet.
0:45 – 0:55 · Scene 4 — The Walk
We never see where they're going. We don't need to.
The 18-year-old walks out her front door. The 35-year-old walks back into her next challenge. Same stride. Same energy. Different stars. The camera pulls back — both women, both skies, both limitless.
0:55 – 1:00 · End card
Black screen. Stars fade in. Text appears.
"For some, sky is the limit. For us — it's just the beginning." · Maybelline Sky High Mascara.
04 — Campaign Faces

One icon. One fresh voice.

The Achiever — The 35-year-old
Smriti Mandhana
Indian cricket's most fearless batter. Universally loved across age groups. She embodies exactly what this campaign stands for — a woman who has already broken through ceilings and is still swinging for more. Her credibility is earned, not manufactured. Her presence tells both audiences: this is who she becomes. This is who she already is.
The Dreamer — The 18-year-old
A fresh face — found, not famous
The younger girl is deliberately not a celebrity. She is found through a real casting call or creator search. She could be anyone. She could be the girl watching this ad. Gen Z doesn't want to see a star they can't become. They want to see themselves — ambitious, real, unpolished, and ready. The contrast with Smriti makes both characters more powerful.
05 — Campaign Executions

Four formats.
One unstoppable idea.

01
Hero Film — 60 sec
"Same Sky, Different Stars"
The cinematic film described above — two women, the same night sky, the same mascara, the same refusal to stop at the sky. Smriti Mandhana as the achiever. A real cast girl as the dreamer.
Moody night cinematography. No voiceover until the final 5 seconds. Distributed across YouTube pre-roll, Instagram Reels, and OTT (Hotstar, Prime Video). The film that starts the conversation.

02
OOH — Billboard Takeover
Eyes on the sky — minimal, powerful
Stark, beautiful billboards across Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, Hyderabad. A close-up of lashes against a deep night-blue sky. The two taglines — one per execution. Nothing else. No cluttered copy. Just confidence.
Metro station placements at eye level — targeting the 28–35 commuting woman every morning. The restraint is the statement. In a sea of maximalist beauty ads, Maybelline shows up with negative space and power.

Malls

03
Creator Campaign
"What do you see when you look at the sky?"
Partner with young Indian influencers Gen Z actually follows. One question: when you look at a sky full of stars — what do you dream of? What inspires you? What are you reaching for? Let them answer in their own voice, raw and unscripted.
20–30 creators across metro and Tier 2 cities. The connection to the campaign is organic — just like in the film, stars represent dreams and ambition. Maybelline becomes the brand that started the conversation about ambition, not just lashes.

04
Social Challenge
#SkyHighDreams — her stars, her story
A UGC campaign asking Indian women: what's your sky full of stars? Share your dream, your ambition, the star you're reaching for. The best stories get featured on Maybelline India's channels.
Maybelline doesn't just sell a mascara — it becomes the brand that amplified her dream. That emotional repositioning is the long game. The product is the symbol. The community is the campaign.
