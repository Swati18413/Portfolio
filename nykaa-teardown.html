01Overview

What is Nykaa?

Nykaa is a digitally native, consumer-first beauty and lifestyle platform that has fundamentally reshaped how beauty products are discovered, evaluated, and purchased in India.

Founded in 2012 by Falguni Nayar, Nykaa began as an online marketplace focused on authentic cosmetics and personal care products — at a time when counterfeit beauty goods and limited brand accessibility were major challenges in the Indian market. What started as a simple e-commerce platform for beauty has since evolved into one of India's most recognisable consumer brands, with over 1 lakh products, 2,000+ brands, and 35 million registered users.

At its core, Nykaa operates as a hybrid commerce platform that combines elements of an inventory-led retail model with a curated marketplace experience. Unlike pure marketplaces that simply connect buyers and sellers, Nykaa takes ownership of inventory for a large portion of its catalogue. This allows it to ensure product authenticity, control quality, and maintain pricing consistency — critical factors in building trust in a category like beauty, where consumer confidence is absolutely essential to conversion.

However, Nykaa is not just an e-commerce platform — it is a content-driven discovery engine. The platform integrates commerce with content through tutorials, influencer collaborations, product reviews, and editorial storytelling. This creates a seamless journey where users move from inspiration to transaction within the same ecosystem. In this sense, Nykaa sits at the intersection of content, community, and commerce — much like global platforms such as Sephora or Ulta Beauty, but deeply localised for the Indian consumer.

Over time, Nykaa has expanded beyond beauty into fashion through Nykaa Fashion, evolving into a broader lifestyle brand. It also operates an omnichannel presence with physical retail stores across major Indian cities — reinforcing its digital-first strategy with offline trust-building touchpoints. From a product perspective, Nykaa solves three core user problems: trust (authentic products), discovery (what to buy), and accessibility (a wide range of brands across price points). Its success lies in tightly integrating these three layers into a single platform — making it not just a place to buy products, but a destination to explore and learn about beauty.

02The Problem

Before Nykaa, beauty
in India was broken.

There was no single platform purpose-built for beauty and personal care in India. Consumers had to rely on fragmented offline stores or horizontal platforms like Amazon — neither of which was optimised for a category as high-involvement as beauty. The experience lacked curation, guidance, and the depth that beauty buyers genuinely need. But the deepest problem wasn't selection. It was trust. Counterfeit products flooded the market. A lipstick bought online might be fake. A serum might be diluted. India's beauty buyer had genuine reason to be afraid — and no platform was meaningfully solving that fear.

1. Lack of a Dedicated Beauty Destination

Before Nykaa, there was no single platform in India that was purpose-built for beauty and personal care. Consumers had to rely on fragmented offline stores or horizontal platforms like Amazon, which were not optimised for beauty discovery. The experience lacked curation, guidance, and category depth. The core insight Nykaa acted on was that beauty is a high-involvement category where users need guidance, trust, and curated choices — not just listings. The opportunity was to build a verticalised platform focused entirely on beauty, combining catalogue depth with guided discovery.

2. Poor Discovery & Unsatisfactory Purchase Experience

Buying beauty products from generic platforms often resulted in unsatisfactory outcomes — wrong shades, unsuitable products, or a complete lack of clarity around usage. There was minimal educational support or contextual recommendation, making decision-making extremely difficult. Nykaa's insight was that beauty buying is not transactional; it is exploratory and requires education — tutorials, reviews, use-cases — embedded directly into the shopping journey. The opportunity was to integrate content into commerce in a way no generic platform had done in India.

3. Limited Accessibility Beyond Metro Cities

Many premium and even mid-range beauty brands were unable to reach Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities due to distribution challenges. This left a large, aspirational user base entirely underserved despite growing demand. Nykaa's insight was that aspiration for beauty is not limited to metros — it is widespread across India. The opportunity was to use e-commerce as a distribution equaliser, unlocking demand in non-metro markets where premium brands had never been accessible before.

4. The Pricing Gap — Premium vs Low Quality

The market had a stark divide — either expensive international brands or cheap, low-quality alternatives with very little in between. A large segment of consumers was willing to pay for quality, but within reasonable limits. Nykaa identified this missing middle in pricing and built a graduated pricing ladder — partnering with brands across price tiers and launching private labels that combined quality with affordability. This expanded the total addressable market while simultaneously improving margins through owned brands.

5. Makeup as Occasion-Driven, Not Everyday

Culturally, makeup in India was largely perceived as something reserved for weddings or special occasions. Daily usage and self-expression through beauty were not mainstream behaviours. Nykaa's insight was that behaviour can be reshaped through education, normalisation, and the right kind of storytelling. Using content, influencers, and editorial narratives, Nykaa repositioned beauty as part of everyday self-expression — expanding its category far beyond what a purely transactional platform could ever achieve.

03Product Strategy

Why Nykaa won —
and why it was deliberate.

Nykaa's success is not accidental — it is the result of a deliberate, well-layered product strategy that combines trust, content, control, and cultural timing. Instead of competing as just another e-commerce platform, Nykaa positioned itself as a beauty-first ecosystem, solving both functional and emotional user needs simultaneously.

1. Trust as the Core Product, Not a Feature

In a market flooded with counterfeit and unreliable products, Nykaa made authenticity its strongest moat. Unlike horizontal platforms like Amazon, Nykaa adopted an inventory-led model, giving it tighter control over sourcing, storage, and delivery. This wasn't just an operational choice — it was a product decision. By guaranteeing authenticity, Nykaa reduced purchase anxiety, especially for first-time buyers experimenting with beauty for the very first time. Trust became a growth engine, not just a hygiene factor — driving repeat purchases and long-term retention in a category that thrives on habit.

Strategic Outcome
Trust became a growth engine, not just a hygiene factor — driving repeat purchases and long-term retention in ways that price-based competition never could.
2. Content + Commerce = Guided Discovery Engine

Nykaa understood early that beauty is not a "search-and-buy" category — it is a learn-and-explore category. Instead of sending users elsewhere for information, it embedded content directly into the platform through tutorials, influencer collaborations, and editorial content. This created a seamless loop: Inspiration → Education → Purchase → Repeat. Globally, players like Sephora and Ulta Beauty operate similarly, but Nykaa localised this deeply for Indian users — featuring Indian skin tones, Indian climate conditions, Indian beauty concerns, and Indian influencers that users actually trusted. The result was higher engagement, longer session times, and improved conversion rates driven by genuinely informed decision-making.

Strategic Outcome
Higher engagement, longer session times, and improved conversion rates — because an informed user is a confident buyer, and a confident buyer returns.
3. Full-Stack Control via Hybrid Model

Nykaa didn't go fully marketplace. Instead, it built a hybrid model — owning inventory for key brands while selectively enabling third-party sellers for catalogue breadth. This allowed Nykaa to maintain quality control, standardise pricing, and ensure a consistent user experience, while still scaling catalogue breadth without the capital intensity of a purely inventory-led model. This balance — control where it matters, flexibility where it helps — is one of Nykaa's most underappreciated strategic decisions.

Strategic Outcome
Balanced control + scalability — avoiding the pitfalls of both pure marketplace (low trust) and pure inventory (unsustainable capital burn).
4. Democratising Beauty Through Smart Pricing Architecture

Nykaa identified the "missing middle" in pricing and actively filled it by partnering with brands across price tiers and launching private labels with strong margins and competitive pricing. Instead of forcing users to choose between cheap and expensive, Nykaa created a graduated pricing ladder — making beauty accessible at every budget while maintaining the aspirational positioning that defines the brand. This expanded the total addressable market while simultaneously improving overall margins through owned brands.

5. Omnichannel Presence to Bridge Trust Gaps

While being digital-first, Nykaa invested in physical retail stores across India. This wasn't just expansion — it was a trust-building mechanism. Offline stores allowed users to experience products physically, build confidence before purchasing online, and experience the brand beyond a screen. For high-consideration beauty categories — skincare, foundation, fragrance — this offline presence reduced the "I need to see it first" barrier that kept many potential customers away from online beauty altogether.

6. Early Bet on Tier 2 & Tier 3 Expansion

Nykaa recognised that aspiration in India is not limited to metros. By leveraging e-commerce logistics, it unlocked demand in smaller cities where premium brands were previously inaccessible. This early bet on geographic expansion gave Nykaa a first-mover advantage in markets that competitors were only beginning to take seriously years later — and captured a user base with both high aspirational demand and genuine willingness to pay for quality.

04Product Strengths

What Nykaa does
genuinely well.

Before diagnosing what's broken, it's important to credit what Nykaa gets right. A product teardown that only finds fault isn't rigorous — it's easy. A strong PM recognises what's working, understands why it works, and builds recommendations on top of real strengths rather than ignoring them.

1. Intuitive & Visually Engaging User Interface

Nykaa offers a highly polished and visually appealing interface that aligns deeply with the nature of the beauty category. The use of high-quality product imagery, clean layouts, and subtle visual cues makes the browsing experience feel premium — not like a generic e-commerce catalogue, but like a curated beauty editorial. More importantly, the interface is not just attractive — it is functional. The information hierarchy is clear: product image, name, price, ratings, offers — all visible at a glance, allowing users to scan and compare products without cognitive overload.

PM Insight
In beauty, visual appeal directly influences purchase decisions. A strong UI helps bridge the gap between digital browsing and the physical product experience that beauty buyers naturally crave. Visual trust precedes purchase trust.
2. Seamless Navigation Across Categories

Nykaa has done a strong job making navigation simple and predictable, both on mobile and desktop. The top navigation — Categories, Brands, Luxe, and more — is well-structured and intuitive. Search is prominently placed and easy to access. Category transitions feel smooth, not jarring. Users can move from broad discovery to a specific product without ever feeling lost in the platform. This reduction in time-to-discovery is a direct driver of conversion rate — and Nykaa has optimised this journey thoughtfully.

3. Highly Effective Left-Side Filtering System

The left panel filtering system is one of Nykaa's strongest UX elements. Filters for Brand, Price, Finish, Skin Type, and Ratings are logically grouped and easy to expand — collapsed by default so they don't overwhelm a first-time user, but readily available to power users who want precision. This design serves both beginners and experts simultaneously, which is a genuinely difficult balance to achieve. It enables progressive narrowing of a very wide catalogue without ever making the experience feel restrictive or complicated.

PM Insight
This is a great example of balancing wide catalogue + controlled discovery. Most platforms make you choose one or the other. Nykaa's filtering system gives you both simultaneously.
4. Smooth Add-to-Cart & Checkout Experience

The purchase journey on Nykaa is optimised for speed and clarity. Adding to cart is simple and visually clear. Cart review is clean and informative without overwhelming the user with choices. The checkout flow is straightforward — minimal steps, no unnecessary interruptions, no dark patterns. Checkout friction is one of the biggest drop-off points in all of e-commerce, and Nykaa has clearly invested in reducing it. This translates directly into higher cart-to-purchase conversion rates.

5. Strong Information Density Without Clutter

Each product card on Nykaa manages to display price, discount, ratings, and offers — without feeling crowded or visually overwhelming. This is a subtle but genuinely important strength. Users receive maximum information with minimum effort, which directly reduces decision fatigue. Getting information density right is one of the hardest problems in product design — too little and users can't decide, too much and they bounce. Nykaa gets this balance right across a very large and complex catalogue.

Strength Synthesis
Nykaa's biggest product strength lies in how it simplifies a highly complex category. Despite offering over one lakh products, the platform ensures users can navigate, evaluate, and purchase with minimal friction. While Nykaa excels in navigation and visual experience, the next opportunity lies in improving decision-making intelligence — not just discovery. That is the gap the recommendations in Section 09 address directly.
05User Personas

Three users.
Three different failure modes.

Nykaa serves a wide range of users — but three distinct personas reveal the product's biggest gaps most clearly. Each one drops off at a different stage of the journey, for a different reason. Understanding these failure modes is precisely where the PM opportunity begins. Good product strategy starts with real people, not abstractions.

R
Riya Sharma
The Beginner Explorer · 18 · College Freshman · Tier 2 City

Riya has never tried any makeup before. She's just started college and sees all the girls around her with flawless skin and premium makeup — and it makes her both curious and a little left out. She decides it's finally time to explore beauty. She opens Nykaa for the first time and is genuinely amazed by the sheer variety — thousands of products, hundreds of brands, every shade imaginable. But that amazement quickly turns to overwhelm. The prices dominating her screen are far beyond a student's budget, and there's no easy way to filter for what she can actually afford. She scrolls for a while, gets confused, and closes the app without buying anything. She comes back a few days later. The same thing happens. Eventually, she downloads Purplle instead.

Goals: Understand what products to buy as a beginner, stay within a student-friendly budget, and avoid making wrong purchase decisions that waste money she doesn't have. Behaviour pattern: High browsing → Low confidence → Drop-off or significantly delayed purchase.

PM Implication: The product lacks a guided entry point for this user. For Riya, the first 60 seconds on Nykaa determines whether she ever converts. This is an activation problem, not a catalogue problem — and activation failures are the most expensive problem to fix retroactively.
N
Natasha
The Trend-Driven Enthusiast · 23 · Digital Marketing Intern · Metro City

Natasha is a frequent Nykaa user. She loves lipsticks — follows every new lip product launch, knows every influencer who talks about them, and gets excited every time a new shade drops. Nykaa is where she buys them. But she has a consistent frustration that's slowly eroding her trust in the platform: the shade shown on the app is almost never the shade that arrives. She picks what looks like a warm nude in the colour palette, places her order confidently, and then receives something that reads entirely differently on her skin. She's returned products multiple times. She's starting to wonder whether it's worth the hassle, or whether she should just buy in a Reliance Tira store where she can see the actual colour before paying.

Goals: Try the latest trending products, achieve accurate shade results, and build a personalised beauty collection that actually works for her skin tone. Behaviour pattern: High intent → Frequent purchases → Frustration from shade mismatch → Slowly eroding trust.

PM Implication: Every wrong shade is a return event, a support ticket, and a reason to try Myntra Beauty or Tira instead. Returns erode LTV, trust, and NPS simultaneously. This is not just a UX problem — it is a retention crisis for high-value users.
T
Tanaya
The Aspiring Influencer · 16 · School Student · Fashionista

Tanaya is in 11th grade but lives and breathes the beauty and fashion world online. She wants to become a social media influencer in beauty and lifestyle — she's already mapping out her content strategy in her head. She installs Nykaa for the first time wanting to build her first complete makeup kit. She has a rough idea of what she wants — something beginner-friendly, affordable, and on-trend — but the platform gives her no structured way to find it. There's no onboarding quiz, no beginner kit builder, no guided flow that says "tell us about yourself and we'll help you start." She's left alone in a catalogue of over one lakh products, with no compass. She eventually orders two random products, feels vaguely unsatisfied, and doesn't come back for two months.

Goals: Build a beginner-friendly makeup kit, learn how to use products effectively, and stay aligned with beauty trends. Behaviour pattern: Very high engagement → Very low initial purchase confidence → Enormous long-term potential if onboarded correctly.

PM Implication: Tanaya is Nykaa's highest-LTV future user. Losing her today to a competitor with better onboarding is not just a conversion loss — it is a decade-long relationship lost. The platform needs to meet aspiring creators at their exact entry point.
06Where It's Breaking

What Nykaa is
getting wrong.

Every strong product has cracks. Nykaa's cracks are real, growing, and in some cases strategically dangerous. Identifying them honestly — not diplomatically — is what separates a genuine PM perspective from a surface-level review.

No Beginner Onboarding — The Activation Gap

First-time users land directly on a catalogue of over one lakh products with zero guidance. There is no onboarding quiz, no "what are you looking for?" prompt, no budget filter at entry, no curated beginner section surfaced during first-use. The assumption baked into the current product is that every user already knows what they want. That assumption excludes a massive segment of high-potential users who are at exactly the moment where guidance could convert them into loyal long-term customers. Riya's drop-off is not about price — it is about the complete absence of a guided entry point.

PM Insight
Activation failure is the most expensive problem to fix retroactively. A user who doesn't convert in their first two sessions is unlikely to return without a specific reason to. Nykaa has no mechanism to bring these users back.
Shade Mismatch — A Slow Trust-Eroder

Nykaa has no AR try-on, no cross-brand shade intelligence, and no skin-tone-based matching system. Users rely on a flat colour palette image and hope that what they see on screen matches what arrives at their door. It frequently doesn't. The problem is compounded across brands — the same "nude" in one brand's language is completely different from "nude" in another's, and Nykaa does nothing to bridge that gap. Users like Natasha absorb these mismatches silently for a while, and then they stop buying makeup from Nykaa altogether. They don't complain loudly. They just leave.

PM Insight
Every wrong shade is a return event, a support ticket, and a quiet trust erosion. Users don't announce their loss of confidence — they simply diversify to platforms that give them more certainty before purchase.
Gen Z is Drifting Away

Gen Z discovers beauty content on Instagram and YouTube. They form preferences, follow creators, and build wishlists — and then they buy somewhere else. Nykaa's content-to-commerce conversion is weakening for this demographic because the platform hasn't evolved to match where Gen Z actually lives online. They don't open an app to browse; they open apps because content pulled them there. Nykaa's editorial approach, which worked brilliantly for Millennials, doesn't speak the same language as the next generation of beauty buyers. The platform is currently winning on content reach and losing on conversion for its most valuable future cohort.

NykaaFashion — Brand Dilution Risk

Expanding into fashion through Nykaa Fashion fragments the brand's core identity. Users trusted Nykaa for one specific thing: being the authority on beauty. That authority came from deep expertise, curated content, trusted partnerships, and years of built credibility in a very specific category. The moment Nykaa tries to be a fashion platform too, that authority begins to blur. It risks becoming another Myntra — a fashion marketplace — rather than remaining the undisputed beauty destination. Brand extension without brand clarity is brand dilution. And diluted brands lose pricing power over time.

Split Deliveries Frustrate Premium Users

Because Nykaa operates a hybrid model — inventory-led for some products, marketplace for others — orders frequently get split across multiple delivery windows. A user who orders five items might receive them in three separate deliveries over four days, with no visibility into which items are arriving when. For casual users, this is mildly annoying. For Nykaa's highest-value, most frequent buyers, it feels disrespectful of their time. Post-purchase experience is the most consistently underestimated retention lever in e-commerce — and Nykaa is leaving significant NPS points on the table here.

07Competitive Landscape

Not just pricing competition —
a battle of experiences.

The Indian beauty e-commerce market has evolved from a simple duopoly into a three-way strategic battle. Nykaa, Purplle, and Tira each represent fundamentally different approaches to winning the same category. Understanding where each player is positioned — and why — is essential to understanding where Nykaa's competitive moat is strongest, and where it is most at risk.

Nykaa occupies the premium, trust-led, content-driven position. Purplle is going after volume through aggressive pricing and private label margins. Tira, backed by Reliance Retail, is positioning itself as a modern, experience-first beauty destination — attempting to replicate a Sephora-like premium experience with the scale advantage of Reliance's offline infrastructure. This is no longer just a pricing competition. It is a battle between three distinct philosophies of what beauty commerce should feel like.

08Tech Stack

Nykaa is not just a beauty app —
it is a data intelligence platform.

Nykaa's tech stack reflects a mature, data-driven e-commerce ecosystem designed to handle three core challenges simultaneously: scale, personalisation, and high-intent commerce journeys. Rather than relying on a minimal setup, Nykaa has adopted a layered architecture that combines cloud infrastructure, big data systems, marketing technology, and modern mobile frameworks — each layer serving a specific product and business goal.

Cloud-First Infrastructure for Scalability

Nykaa leverages cloud services like Amazon Web Services — including Route 53 and Redshift — to ensure high availability and scalability across the platform. This allows it to handle massive traffic spikes during its biggest conversion moments: the Pink Friday Sale, the Hot Pink Sale, and major brand launch events. Services can be scaled independently, maintaining low latency across geographies even when demand surges. The PM insight here is direct: for Nykaa, downtime during a sale is not a technical failure — it is a revenue failure, measured in crores per hour.

Data-Driven Architecture — The Real Backbone

With tools like Apache Hadoop, Apache Spark, Hive, and Amazon Redshift, Nykaa has invested heavily in a big data infrastructure that powers user behaviour tracking, recommendation systems, demand forecasting, and personalisation at scale. This is what makes Nykaa not just an e-commerce platform, but a data intelligence platform that continuously learns from how its users browse, compare, and buy. The multi-database strategy — MySQL and PostgreSQL for structured data, MongoDB for flexible catalogue and content data, and Neo4j for relationship-based data that powers recommendations — reflects the genuine complexity of a beauty catalogue where variants like shades, skin types, and finishes need to be handled with precision.

PM Insight
The multi-database architecture enables smarter product discovery and more precise recommendations — two capabilities that directly impact conversion in a high-consideration category like beauty.
Event-Driven & Asynchronous Systems

With tools like RabbitMQ, Nykaa uses event-driven architecture to handle order processing, notification systems, inventory updates, and background jobs like recommendations and analytics — all without blocking the user experience. This ensures that even when backend processes are complex (which in a hybrid inventory-plus-marketplace model they frequently are), the front-end experience remains fast and responsive.

Marketing & Ad-Tech Stack

Nykaa integrates a sophisticated marketing technology stack including Adobe Marketing Cloud, AppNexus, The Trade Desk, and Facebook Conversion Tracking. These systems power targeted advertising, retargeting campaigns, and user acquisition funnels with precision attribution. Growth on Nykaa is not accidental — it is engineered through precision targeting and rigorous attribution systems that continuously optimise CAC and acquisition ROI. The CRM layer, powered by Salesforce and Oracle, manages customer journeys, retention campaigns, and the Nykaa Prive loyalty programme.

Search, Discovery, and Personalisation

Search on Nykaa is not simply keyword-based — it needs to support both intent-based and exploratory discovery in ways that generic platforms like Amazon do not. This means semantic search that understands queries like "matte lipstick for oily skin in summer", advanced filtering by skin type, tone, concern, brand, and price, and ranking algorithms that balance popularity, reviews, and personal relevance. The personalisation layer uses browsing history, purchase data, and trending signals to surface relevant products on the homepage, in push notifications, and in curated shelves — improving engagement, increasing basket size, and driving the kind of repeat purchase behaviour that builds Nykaa's long-term revenue.

Modern Mobile Architecture

Nykaa's mobile strategy reflects strong modernisation: Android uses MVA architecture with Jetpack, moving away from legacy XML-based layouts, while iOS uses MVVM with SwiftUI. This means faster UI development, better runtime performance, and a cleaner, more maintainable codebase — all of which translate directly into a faster, more personalised, and more reliable experience for users who interact with Nykaa primarily through their phones. Mobile is not just a channel for Nykaa — it is the product.

09What I Would Build

Moving Nykaa from product
marketplace to decision platform.

Every recommendation below targets a specific friction point identified through the persona analysis, and is tied to a specific user. I am not optimising for isolated vanity metrics like page views or app opens. I am optimising for decision confidence — because a user who buys confidently returns. A user who guesses, returns the product, and may never return at all.

i.Budget-Led Discovery Layer
Persona → Riya · Beginner Explorer
The Gap

Users perceive Nykaa as expensive and drop off early — despite the fact that affordable options genuinely exist within the catalogue. This is not a catalogue problem. It is a discovery and framing problem. The platform fails to surface budget-appropriate options proactively, and makes no attempt to understand what a first-time user can afford before exposing them to a catalogue dominated by premium pricing.

What I Would Build

I would introduce a Budget-First Discovery Layer integrated directly into search and onboarding. Concretely: a price selector placed prominently next to the search bar — Under ₹500, ₹500–₹1,000, or Premium — so users can set their context before they even begin browsing. For first-time users, I would add a quick onboarding prompt: "What's your budget today?" The homepage would then dynamically adjust to surface budget-friendly collections, best picks under a specific price point, and curated entry-level sets. I would also add visual affordability cues throughout the catalogue — badges like "Student Pick" and "Best Value" — that normalise affordable choices without making them feel like a downgrade. The goal is to make Nykaa feel welcoming at every budget, not aspirational only at premium price points.

ii.AR Virtual Try-On + Universal Shade Intelligence
Persona → Natasha · Beauty Enthusiast
The Gap

The shade shown on the app is not the shade that arrives. There is no AR try-on, no cross-brand shade mapping, and no system that accounts for individual skin tone or undertone. Users like Natasha have learned to distrust the colour palette images on product pages — and this distrust is slowly converting them into Tira or Myntra Beauty customers.

What I Would Build

I would build two connected systems. First, an AR-powered virtual try-on for lipsticks, foundation, and blush — camera-based, with real-time shade switching and lighting adjustments for day and night conditions. Every product page would carry a "Try this shade" CTA that activates this experience with one tap. Second, a Universal Shade Intelligence System: when a user uploads a selfie, the system detects their skin tone and undertone — warm, cool, or neutral — and builds a personal shade profile. When that user browses across different brands, the system would automatically surface their match: "Your shade in this brand is X." This profile would improve continuously using purchase data, returns data, and direct user feedback. The two systems together would reduce the core anxiety of buying makeup online — the uncertainty of not knowing what it will actually look like on you.

iii.Smart Kit Builder
Persona → Riya + Tanaya · Beginners
The Gap

Users don't fail because they don't want to buy. They fail because they don't know what to buy together. A lipstick needs a liner. A foundation needs a primer. A skincare routine needs to be sequenced correctly. For beginners, the absence of this guidance is the difference between a confident first purchase and a confusing one that doesn't come back for weeks.

What I Would Build

I would build a Smart Kit Builder — a guided quiz that asks users three simple questions: What is your skin type? What is your budget? What is the occasion? Based on the answers, the system would generate a complete kit of five to eight products, with a brief explanation for each — "Why this product?" — that builds confidence rather than just presenting a list. Users could customise the kit, swap out individual products, and add everything to cart in a single tap. Kit categories would include a Beginner Kit, Wedding Kit, Skincare Routine, Gifting Kit, and a Creator Starter Kit specifically designed for aspiring influencers like Tanaya. The goal is to shift Nykaa from a platform that sells products to one that delivers complete solutions.

High-value users who order multiple products receive them across three days in two or three separate shipments, with no advance information about which items are coming when. The best products don't make exits difficult — they make the experience good enough that users choose to stay. Giving delivery control is one way to earn that choice.
